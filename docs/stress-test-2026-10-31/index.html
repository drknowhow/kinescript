Kinescript Stress Test

Four bug hunts, one per area of the engine, a 23-scene stress benchmark, and a profile of the slowest scenes. This page lists every finding with where it stands, the benchmark numbers, and the performance work ranked by what the profiles showed.

Conditions

How the numbers were taken

Everything ran on one Windows 11 machine: Intel64 Family 6 Model 198 with 20 threads, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5060 Ti (driver 610.88), Python 3.14.4, skia-python 144.1, numpy 2.4.4 and ffmpeg 8.1.2. Rendering used the GPU backend.

The machine was shared. A local Ollama server held about 14.4 of the GPU's 16.3 GB, and other processes used about 18% of the CPU. Stage times are medians over 5 runs. Stress rows are single runs; a second run of text-split, sequence-60, models-30 and expressions came back within a few percent.

Performance

Where the time goes, ranked

Ranked by measured cost against the size of the change. The evidence comes from cProfile on the GPU backend and from the tables above. The first two have a measured gain; the rest have a measured cost and an untested remedy. Proposals 1, 2 and 5 of the earlier Cod review appear here with their numbers. Its proposal 3, warm worker processes, is already on main, and proposal 4, caching static subtrees, was not measured.

  1. Encode PNG frames faster

    output._render_pngs, output.still, verify.contact_sheet
    Evidence
    The png-seq scene (1 s of hello.json as PNG frames) took 11.74 s for 30 frames. Per frame, drawing took 5 ms and Skia's PNG encoder took 376 ms, measured on three frames. The same encoder writes every kine still and kine look image; the 7680x4320 still took 4.6 s.
    Change
    Encode in the existing worker pool, and write the PNG with zlib directly. A numpy and zlib writer with no row filter took 91 ms at zlib level 6 (1.40 MB per frame against Skia's 1.18 MB) and 30 ms at level 1 (2.83 MB).
    Gain
    About 4x from the encoder alone, multiplied by the worker count.
    Effort
    Small

  2. Put layer opacity in the paint instead of a saveLayer

    render.py layer drawing
    Evidence
    A prototype (local branch perf-proto-savelayer, ddf6d0d) took the expressions scene from 32.8 to 14.0 ms per frame and layers-2000 from 30.9 to 24.9 ms. The largest pixel difference was 3/255.
    Change
    When a layer with opacity below 1 is a single draw with no children, blend mode or effects, set the alpha on its paint and skip the offscreen layer.
    Gain
    2.3x on expression-heavy scenes, 1.2x on many plain shapes.
    Effort
    Small to medium; needs a pixel-tolerance test beside the purity test.

  3. Draw split text without a layer per character

    render._draw_text
    Evidence
    text-split ran at 13.8 fps (115 ms per frame). readPixels waited 54 ms per frame for the GPU to finish 333 per-character saveLayers with blur, and _draw_text spent about 59 ms of Python on 2,560 drawString calls a frame, each with its own save and restore.
    Change
    Draw characters that have no per-unit effect as one text blob, and skip the blur layer when the blur is 0.
    Gain
    Not measured. The two costs found add up to almost the whole frame.
    Effort
    Medium

  4. Keep decoders open while check samples a sequence

    verify.check, media decoder
    Evidence
    check on sequence-60 took 8.45 s against 11.06 s for the full render. About 75% of it was waiting on ffmpeg: about 98 decoder processes were started, and decoder reads came to 9.6 s cumulative.
    Change
    Reuse one decoder per clip across samples and sample in time order, or skip decoding for the rules that only need layer boxes.
    Gain
    Not measured. Most of the 8.45 s is decoder start-up and reads.
    Effort
    Medium

  5. Vectorize the 3D model draw

    render3d.draw
    Evidence
    models-30 ran at 36.8 fps on 4 workers (86 ms per frame in one process). render3d.draw cost 1.2 ms of Python per model per frame (1,320 calls, 1.63 s self time), plus Skia MakeCopy and numpy work. The model workload's draw time was 10.17 ms median and 43.23 ms at p95.
    Change
    Project and shade each model's triangles in one numpy pass and draw them as one vertices call.
    Gain
    Not measured.
    Effort
    Medium

  6. Accumulate motion-blur samples on the GPU

    render.py motion blur
    Evidence
    The blur workload's frame time was 8.27 ms median against 2.74 ms for hello.
    Change
    Add sub-frames into a GPU accumulation surface instead of compositing each one at full precision (proposal 2 in the earlier Cod review).
    Gain
    Not measured.
    Effort
    Medium to large

  7. Read frames back asynchronously

    render.py readback, output.py pipe
    Evidence
    On hello, the full frame took 2.74 ms against 1.40 ms of drawing; readback and conversion were about 1.3 ms and blocking on the ffmpeg pipe about 0.46 ms.
    Change
    Double-buffer the readback so the next frame draws while the last one is read (proposal 1 in the Cod review).
    Gain
    At most about 1.8 ms per frame, which helps only simple scenes. long-120s already runs at 462 fps on 4 workers.
    Effort
    Medium

  8. Keep decoded video on the GPU

    media decoder, render.py video layers
    Evidence
    The edit workload's draw time was 9.46 ms median and 23.56 ms at p95, against 1.40 ms for hello.
    Change
    Upload decoded frames once and keep them as GPU textures (proposal 5 in the Cod review).
    Gain
    Not measured.
    Effort
    Large
