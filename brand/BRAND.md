# Kinescript brand guide

The direction is called **Kinetic Score**: the brand looks like the score a scene is played from. That means
timing bars, cues placed on them, and the eased paths between cues. Everything is drawn in code, from the same
geometry the renderer uses.

## Name

**Kinescript**, always one word, capital K in prose. The wordmark is lowercase: `kinescript`.
The command is `kine`. From *kine-* (motion) and *script*: motion that is written down.

## Line

- Tagline: **Video you can diff.**
- One-sentence description: Motion graphics and video edits written as JSON, checked by agents, rendered on the GPU.
- Longer: Kinescript turns a JSON scene into video. Every layer, keyframe and cut is data, so a scene can be
  generated, reviewed in a pull request and changed by an agent, then checked and rendered from the command line.

## What we say about it

Say what it does and show the number. Each claim below has a measurement or a command behind it.

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Any frame renders on its own | the renderer takes a frame number and the scene, nothing else (`tests/test_core.py::test_frame_is_pure_function_of_index`) |
| Times can reference other layers | `"at": "title.out+0.2"` |
| It checks its own output | `kine check` reports layout, contrast, size and reading-time problems with time ranges |
| An agent can review it without watching | `kine look` produces one image of many frames |
| It is fast on a GPU | launch video: 34.6 s of 1080p rendered with audio in about 6.4 s (about 160 frames/s, median of 5 runs); 5.2 ms median per frame |
| It is small | three Python dependencies plus ffmpeg |

Do not compare Kinescript with named products in marketing copy. Describe it on its own terms.

## Voice

Plain and exact. Short declarative sentences. Numbers instead of adjectives. Dry rather than excited.

- Yes: "Lengthen one layer and everything cued after it moves."
- Yes: "35 seconds of 1080p, rendered in 6."
- No: "Revolutionary", "blazing fast", "seamless", "next-gen", "magic".
- No exclamation marks. No emoji in product copy.

## Colour

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Void | `#0A0C10` | backgrounds, text on light |
| Sheet | `#F2F0E8` | primary text and strokes on dark, light backgrounds |
| Track | `#242832` | timing lines, rules, panel borders |
| Cue | `#FF633F` | the cue dot, emphasis, one accent per frame |
| Keyframe | `#B9F45D` | second accent: code values, success, keyframe squares |
| Annotation | `#9299A8` | secondary text, captions, labels |

Cue is for one thing per frame. When a second thing needs emphasis it uses Keyframe. Panels on Void use
`#12151B` with a Track border.

These are the defaults in `kinescript/components.py` (`THEME`, under both the names above and the short names
`ink`, `paper`, `accent`, `accent2`, `muted`), so components follow the brand without settings.

## Type

- **Space Grotesk** for headlines and text (bundled, SIL OFL). Headlines Bold (700), body Regular (400) or
  Medium (500). The wordmark is Space Grotesk Bold with -3 px tracking at 128 px.
- **IBM Plex Mono** (bundled, SIL OFL) for code, commands, timecodes and data.
- Headlines in sentence case, ending with a full stop when they are statements: "Write it as data."

## Logo

The mark is a **K made of timing**: a vertical bar (a track), one eased interpolation path for the arms, and a
Cue dot where the path turns. It is a bar, a curve and a circle, and nothing else, so it holds up at 16 px.

Files in this folder, regenerated by `python brand/make_logo.py` (text is outlined, no font needed to open them):

- `logo-dark.svg/png` (Sheet on Void), `logo-light.svg/png` (Void on Sheet), `logo-transparent.svg/png`
- `mark.svg/png`, `mark-light.svg/png`: the K alone
- `icon.svg/png`: the K on a Void rounded square, for app icons and avatars
- `favicon-16/32/180.png`: the icon with heavier strokes for small sizes

Rules: keep clear space of one dot diameter on every side. Do not recolour the dot. The arms are one continuous
curve; do not straighten them or break them apart. The minimum width of the full logo is 120 px.

## Art

`art/` holds generated plates (made for this brand, no text in them): `hero.jpg` (empty left side for copy),
`og.jpg` (link card background), `transition.jpg` (centred cue, for section breaks and panels) and `texture.jpg`
(a very faint grid, for large flat areas). Text always goes on top as real type; never generate type into an
image. `docs/og.png` is rendered from `marketing/og.json`.

## Motion: score-build

The brand moves the way the mark is built.

1. Timing bars and rules land first: `draw` or `wipe-right` with `outQuart`, 0.3 to 0.4 s.
2. Cues snap onto them: `snap` (0.2 to 0.3 s) for text and panels, `pop` from 0.2 for dots.
3. Paths draw between cues: `draw`, 0.4 to 0.5 s.

Headlines enter with a short `left` move (28 px, 0.32 s). Letter stagger is about 0.03 s. Sections end on a hard
cut or a 0.2 s fade. No blur entrances and no drifting gradients. The background is the `score` shader (beat
lines, bars every four beats, registration crosses) that moves in whole beats; vignette 0.3, grain 0.03.
