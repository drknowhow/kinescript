# Kinescript brand guide

## Name

**Kinescript**, always one word, capital K in prose. The wordmark is lowercase: `kinescript`.
The command is `kine`. From *kine-* (motion) and *script*: motion that is written down.

## Line

- Tagline: **Video you can diff.**
- One-sentence description: Motion graphics and video edits written as JSON, checked by agents, rendered on the GPU.
- Longer: Kinescript turns a JSON scene into video. Every layer, keyframe and cut is data, so a scene can be
  generated, reviewed in a pull request and changed by an agent, then checked and rendered from the command line.

## What we say about it

Say what it does and show the number. Each claim below has a measurement or a command behind it.

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Any frame renders on its own | the renderer takes a frame number and the scene, nothing else (`tests/test_core.py::test_frame_is_pure_function_of_index`) |
| Times can reference other layers | `"at": "title.out+0.2"` |
| It checks its own output | `kine check` reports layout, contrast, size and reading-time problems with time ranges |
| An agent can review it without watching | `kine look` produces one image of many frames |
| It is fast on a GPU | launch video: 34.4 s of 1080p rendered in 7.2 s with audio; 5.1 ms median per frame |
| It is small | three Python dependencies plus ffmpeg |

Do not compare Kinescript with named products in marketing copy. Describe it on its own terms.

## Voice

Plain and exact. Short declarative sentences. Numbers instead of adjectives. Dry rather than excited.

- Yes: "Lengthen one layer and everything cued after it moves."
- Yes: "34 seconds of 1080p, rendered in 7."
- No: "Revolutionary", "blazing fast", "seamless", "next-gen", "magic".
- No exclamation marks. No emoji in product copy.

## Colour

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#0E0F13` | backgrounds, text on light |
| Paper | `#F3EFE6` | primary text on dark, light backgrounds |
| Cue | `#FF5A36` | the dot, emphasis, one accent per frame |
| Signal | `#3DDBD9` | secondary accent: code values, success, data |
| Graphite | `#8B8F99` | secondary text, captions, rules |
| Panel | `#16181F` | cards and code panels on Ink |

Cue is for one thing per frame. When two things need emphasis, the second uses Signal.
These are the defaults in `kinescript/components.py` (`THEME`), so components follow the brand without settings.

## Type

- **Inter** for everything (bundled, SIL OFL). Headlines ExtraBold (800), body Regular (400) or Medium (500).
- **Cascadia Code** (SIL OFL) for code, commands and data in video and on the site; fall back to any monospace.
- Headlines in sentence case, ending with a full stop when they are statements: "Write it as data."

## Logo

The mark is the **cue dot**: a Cue-coloured circle with three motion lines trailing to its left. In film, a cue
dot in the corner of the frame tells the projectionist a change is coming. Here it marks a frame that was planned.

Files in this folder, regenerated by `python brand/make_logo.py` (text is outlined, no font needed to open them):

- `logo-dark.svg/png` (Paper on Ink), `logo-light.svg/png` (Ink on Paper), `logo-transparent.svg/png`
- `mark.svg/png` and `mark-light.svg/png`: the dot and lines alone, for avatars and favicons

Rules: keep clear space of one dot diameter on every side. Do not recolour the dot. Do not separate the motion
lines from the dot. The minimum width of the full logo is 120 px.

## Motion

The brand moves the way the tool's defaults move: `rise` with a light blur for words, `pop` with `outBack` for the
dot, `draw` for lines. Durations of 0.4 to 0.6 s, letter stagger 0.03 s. Sections cut with a 0.35 s fade.
Background: the `mesh` shader at 10 to 15 % opacity, vignette 0.35 to 0.4, grain 0.03 to 0.04.
