Characters that talk.

An avatar is a character drawn once, as SVG parts or as a glTF model, in a folder with an avatar.json. A scene gives it a line, and its mouth follows the words. It blinks, breathes and glances on its own, and smiles, points and looks where the scene says, each on a cue.

The built-in host presents a chart

"avatar": "kine:host" is the starter: a plain figure in the scene's palette with all nine mouth shapes, speaking with Kokoro. The line is text in the scene; kine voice speaks it, and its marked words cue the chart, the glance at it, the point and the surprise.

A presenter from generated pictures

Maya began as fourteen pictures from an image model: one portrait, then edits of it that change only the mouth or the eyes. kine avatar from-images keyed out the green background, lined each picture up with the portrait, and turned what each one changed into a patch over it. She talks with nine mouth shapes, blinks, looks at the title, and smiles and looks surprised on cue.

Reactions that show on cue

Rin came from fifteen generated pictures, like Maya, plus four effects drawn as SVG: sparkles, a sweat drop, an anger mark and surprise lines. A part marked "effect": true stays hidden until an expression or gesture names it, so each one shows on its word and goes again.

Two drawn characters, talking to each other

Cue is the Kinescript mascot: a robot whose mouth is a waveform and whose antenna pings. Slate is a clapperboard whose clapper is its jaw, and on "cut" its board says so. Both are SVG parts drawn by brand/make_mascots.py, coloured by palette roles so a brand recolours them. Each line starts at a cue in the other's: "at": "cue-say1.out+0.3".

A 3D avatar from a glTF file

Its mouth shapes, blink and expressions are the model's morph targets by name, its gestures are the model's animation clips, and a gaze turns the model. This head is generated by examples/make_media.py, so the example needs no downloaded model.

Every pose on one sheet: kine look FOLDER

An agent cannot watch a mouth move, so the sheet shows each mouth shape (and what a missing one falls back to), the eyes closed and looking aside, each expression, and each gesture where it moves most. A broken pivot or a missing shape shows here before a render. kine avatar check FOLDER lists the same problems as text.

Mouths from words
A voice's word times become mouth shapes by spelling rules, with Kokoro's phonemes where it gives them. kine probe --mouth shows the track; kine check says when a line has no word times and the mouth only follows loudness.
Idle life
Blinks, breathing and small glances, timed from the layer's id and seed, so every render of a frame is the same. No blink lands as a line starts or at a gesture's peak.
Acting on cues
expressions, gestures and gaze are lists on the layer, each at a cue such as "host-say:third". Kinescript checks them; you write them.
From pictures
kine avatar from-images FOLDER turns a portrait and edits of it (a blink, mouth shapes, looks and expressions) into an avatar. --init writes the prompts for each picture, and the report names any picture that changed more than it should.
Effects
A part with "effect": true is hidden until an expression or gesture names it: sparkles, a sweat drop, motion lines. fx-NAME.svg next to the pictures becomes one.
Your own avatar
kine avatar new FOLDER copies the host as SVG parts to redraw. Colours can be palette roles, so an avatar takes the scene's brand, and a brand can name avatars and their voices.
Recorded speech
say also takes a sound in audio, recorded or spoken, with a words file, so a real voice-over drives the mouth too.
Cost
A drawn avatar costs little more than a still picture of it. A 3D head costs what any model of its size costs, moving or not.
